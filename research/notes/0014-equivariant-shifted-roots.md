## The source-licensed shift orbit

Retain every analytic and parameter hypothesis of Notes 0012--0013. Work on
the four-dimensional periodic fine lattice \(\mathbb T^{(k)}\), with
fine-coordinate side length \(N\). Let \(L>1\) be the RG block factor and let
the standard localization length, in current fine-lattice units, be

\[
M=L^m,
\qquad m\ge2,
\tag{1}
\]

with \(M\mid N\). The sentence preceding RG I p. 269 Eq. (2.17) restricts
the Euclidean symmetry \(r\) to one preserving the next coarse lattice
\(\mathbb T^{(k+1)}\); Eq. (2.17) defines its pullback on fields, and the
discussion after Eqs. (2.17)--(2.18) gives the induced split and fluctuation
transformation. This source chain supplies covariance of the undecoupled RG
construction. Its allowed translations are multiples of \(L\), not arbitrary
unit translations in the current fine coordinates. Equivariance of the
intermediate weakening is the separate repository construction below.

Set

\[
S_{M,L}=(L\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^4,
\qquad
n=\frac ML=L^{m-1}>1,
\tag{2}
\]

and let \(\pi_k^\sigma\), for \(\sigma\in S_{M,L}\), be the translate of the
standard periodic \(M\)-cube partition whose coordinate walls have residues
\(\sigma_i\) modulo \(M\). Every such shift is generated by a translation
preserving \(\mathbb T^{(k+1)}\). No unit-shifted RG branch is imported here.

For a fine plaquette \(p\), define

\[
\mathcal A_{M,L}(p)=
\{\sigma\in S_{M,L}:p\text{ lies strictly inside one cube of }
\pi_k^\sigma\}.
\tag{3}
\]

For coordinate direction \(i\), let \(f_i(p)\) be the number of allowed wall
residues in \(L\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z\) that meet the geometric plaquette in
that direction. An elementary plaquette has coordinate extent zero or one,
whereas distinct allowed wall residues are separated by \(L>1\). Hence

\[
f_i(p)\in\{0,1\},
\qquad
n_p:=|\mathcal A_{M,L}(p)|
=\prod_{i=1}^4\bigl(n-f_i(p)\bigr)
\ge(n-1)^4>0.
\tag{4}
\]

Thus every elementary plaquette has at least one root in the admitted shift
orbit, including plaquettes crossing walls of the original partition. Unlike
the all-unit-shift count, \(n_p\) can depend on the position of \(p\) relative
to the next coarse lattice. The normalization below uses its actual value.
Equation (4) is repository periodic-lattice bookkeeping, not a printed
Bałaban constant.

## An equivariant weakening choice

RG I Eq. (2.17) does not itself state covariance of RG II's chosen
generalized-random-walk expansion at intermediate weakening variables. Fix
that gap before defining the shifted activities.

Let \(\mathsf H\) be the finite stabilizer of the standard partition
\(\pi_k^0\) inside the coarse-lattice-preserving group. For each covariant
propagator entering RG II Eqs. (1.3)--(1.4), write one of the paper's
generalized-random-walk expansions as a sum of localized terms
\(\sum_\omega\mathcal R_\omega\). Replace it by

\[
\mathcal R_\omega^{\rm eq}(U,J)
=\frac1{|\mathsf H|}
\sum_{h\in\mathsf H}
h_*\!\left[
\mathcal R_{h^{-1}\omega}(h_*^{-1}U,h_*^{-1}J)
\right].
\]

Here \(h_*\) is geometric pushforward, expressed in the paper's pullback
convention by taking \(r=h^{-1}\):

\[
h_*U:=rU,
\qquad
(h_*U)(hb)=U(b),
\]

with the analogous action on \(J\), operator kernels, and the orthogonal
fluctuation variables described after RG I Eqs. (2.17)--(2.18).

Each summand, after summing over \(\omega\), reconstructs the same exact
covariant propagator. The averaged terms therefore still sum to that
propagator. Their supports remain localized, the printed analytic estimates
remain valid by the triangle inequality, and setting wall-crossing monomials
to zero remains block diagonal. The new expansion is exactly
\(\mathsf H\)-equivariant.

For \(\sigma\in S_{M,L}\), choose a translation \(\tau_\sigma\) with
\(\tau_\sigma\pi_k^0=\pi_k^\sigma\) and transport the averaged expansion by
\(\tau_\sigma\). The result is independent of the representative: two choices
differ by an element of \(\mathsf H\). If \(g\) preserves the next coarse
lattice, then
\(\tau_{g\sigma}^{-1}g\tau_\sigma\in\mathsf H\). Consequently the weakened
propagators intertwine under \((g_*s)(g\Delta)=s(\Delta)\). Relabeling the
independent-variable reconstruction and local RG-II inequalities, covariance
of the local nonlinear maps, and uniqueness of the RG-II fixed points in Eqs.
(1.3)--(1.4) give the repository corollary

\[
H_k^{g\sigma}(g_*s,g_*B')=g_*H_k^\sigma(s,B').
\]

This finite stabilizer average is a repository construction. It is not claimed
to be the particular generalized-random-walk expansion printed by Bałaban.
It changes neither the undecoupled reconstruction nor the analytic bound used
in Notes 0012--0013.

## The shifted family

For each \(\sigma\in\mathcal A_{M,L}(p)\), use the equivariant weakened
reconstruction above and define the shifted version of Note 0012 Eq. (4),

\[
F_p^\sigma(s;U,J,B)=
\Phi_p(U,H_k^\sigma(s,B'(B)))
-\Phi_p(U,H_k^\sigma(s,B'(0))).
\]

Apply Note 0012's finite mixed differences to this function. Denote the
resulting activities by

\[
W_{k,p}^\sigma(Y;U,J,B),
\qquad Y\supset Q_{p,\sigma},
\tag{5}
\]

where \(Q_{p,\sigma}\) is the unique root cube and \(Y\) is a connected union
of cubes of \(\pi_k^\sigma\). The analytic domain on a shifted \(Y\) means the
transport of the same local inequalities appearing in RG II Eq. (1.34), not a
new or larger domain.

Turning every weakening variable on removes the decoupling walls. The averaged
random-walk terms sum to the exact propagators, and the RG-I pre-/post-Eqs.
(2.17)--(2.18) source chain cited above then gives, for every admitted shift,
the same undecoupled mark:

\[
\Delta_p(U,J,B)
=\sum_{Y\supset Q_{p,\sigma}}W_{k,p}^\sigma(Y;U,J,B).
\tag{6}
\]

Define

\[
\widehat W_{k,p}(\sigma,Y;U,J,B)
=\frac1{n_p}W_{k,p}^\sigma(Y;U,J,B).
\tag{7}
\]

Averaging (6) over the nonempty set (3) proves

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_p(U,J,B)=
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal A_{M,L}(p)}
\sum_{Y\supset Q_{p,\sigma}}
\widehat W_{k,p}(\sigma,Y;U,J,B).
}
\tag{8}
\]

Equation (8) is an exact finite-cover decomposition for every plaquette. It is
still a statement about the selected RG-coordinate branch.

## No additional orbit entropy

Let

\[
a=(1-2\delta)\kappa,
\qquad
q_d=\frac{64e^a}{e^{\kappa_1}-1},
\qquad
\mathcal B_d=
\frac{C_{\rm mark}e^{16\kappa_1}\varepsilon_1}{1-q_d}.
\tag{9}
\]

Retain Note 0013's source-safe consequence
\(\kappa_1-1\ge(1-\delta)\kappa\), the range
\(0<\delta<1/2\), and the explicit repository margin
\(\delta\kappa>\log64\). Then \(q_d<1\).

Let \(d_{k,\sigma}\) be the translate of RG I p. 257's contained
shortest-tree metric on unions of cubes of \(\pi_k^\sigma\). Apply Note 0013
on each transported branch and use (7):

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sup_p
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal A_{M,L}(p)}
\sum_{Y\supset Q_{p,\sigma}}
e^{a d_{k,\sigma}(Y)}
\sup_{\text{transported Eq. (1.34) domain on }Y}
|\widehat W_{k,p}(\sigma,Y)|\\
&\qquad\le
\sup_p\frac1{n_p}
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal A_{M,L}(p)}\mathcal B_d
=\mathcal B_d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

The normalized cover therefore pays no additional orbit factor. An
unnormalized sum over the \(n_p\) admissible shifts would pay \(n_p\). For a
finite profile, linearity gives the safe bound
\(\mathcal B_d\sum_p|f_p|\).

## Coarse-lattice-preserving covariance

Let \(\Gamma_{N,L}\) be the periodic hypercubic symmetry class fixed
immediately before RG I Eq. (2.17): the Euclidean symmetries of
\(\mathbb T^{(k)}\) that preserve \(\mathbb T^{(k+1)}\). In coordinates write

\[
g(x)=P_gx+t_g,
\qquad
t_g\in(L\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^4,
\qquad
g\sigma=P_g\sigma+t_g\pmod M,
\tag{11}
\]

where \(P_g\) is a signed coordinate permutation. Equivalently,
\(g\pi_k^\sigma=\pi_k^{g\sigma}\). The action gives bijections

\[
g\mathcal A_{M,L}(p)=\mathcal A_{M,L}(gp),
\qquad
n_{gp}=n_p,
\qquad
d_{k,g\sigma}(gY)=d_{k,\sigma}(Y).
\tag{12}
\]

Use the source convention through geometric pushforward: for \(r=g^{-1}\),
set

\[
g_*U:=rU,
\qquad
(g_*U)(gb)=U(b),
\qquad
(g_*s)(g\Delta)=s(\Delta),
\tag{13}
\]

with analogous notation for \(J\) and for the induced orthogonal
fluctuation-field action on \(B\) described after RG I Eqs. (2.17)--(2.18).
The equivariant weakening construction, the cited undecoupled source
transformation, and relabeling invariance of the local RG-II reconstruction
and inequalities preserve the transported Eq. (1.34) domain. They yield the
following repository marked corollary between equivariantly transported
charts:

\[
F_{gp}^{g\sigma}(g_*s;g_*U,g_*J,g_*B)
=F_p^\sigma(s;U,J,B).
\tag{14}
\]

Turning cube variables on or off and taking mixed differences commute with
this relabeling. Therefore

\[
W_{k,gp}^{g\sigma}
(gY;g_*U,g_*J,g_*B)
=W_{k,p}^{\sigma}(Y;U,J,B),
\tag{15}
\]

and the same holds for \(\widehat W\) because of (12).

For a reflection, canonical rebasing and reorientation make the transported
plaquette holonomy conjugate either to \(X_p\) or to \(X_p^{-1}\). Conjugacy
invariance of trace and
\(\operatorname{Tr}(X^{-1})=\operatorname{Tr}(X)\) for
\(X\in SL(2,\mathbb C)\) prove (14) for the holomorphic outer function. This
step is specific to \(SU(2)\) and its \(SL(2,\mathbb C)\) complexification; it
is not asserted for higher-rank groups.

Equations (8), (10), and (15) give an exact
\(\Gamma_{N,L}\)-equivariant shifted-family representation. Although (8)
covers every plaquette, (15) does not cover unit translations that move the
fixed next-coarse lattice. Full fine-lattice space-group covariance, and
continuous Euclidean symmetry in a continuum limit, remain open.

## Exact boundary

- The theorem is conditional on Notes 0012--0013, \(M=L^m\) with \(m\ge2\),
  the RG-I coarse-lattice-preserving symmetry class preceding Eq. (2.17),
  equivariantly transported charts, and the same strict local analytic
  inequalities and entropy margin.
- Equation (15) uses the explicit finite stabilizer average above; the
  pre-Eq. (2.17) symmetry restriction, Eq. (2.17) field pullback, and
  post-Eqs. (2.17)--(2.18) induced fluctuation transformation for the
  undecoupled construction; fixed-point uniqueness; and relabeling invariance
  of the independent-variable reconstruction and stated local domain. The
  marked and intermediate weakened intertwining is a repository corollary,
  not a theorem quoted from the paper, and says nothing about arbitrary
  unit-shifted RG schemes.
- The reflection argument is restricted to \(SU(2)\) and
  \(SL(2,\mathbb C)\).
- The output is a family indexed by \((\sigma,Y)\), not one polymer family on
  a single fixed partition. A later cluster expansion cannot erase the shift
  label or mix its unmarked decorations without a compatibility theorem.
- The result remains on the selected RG-coordinate branch. It proves no
  identification with the intrinsic coarea kernel or unrestricted raw law and
  no compatibility of unrelated background gauge charts.
- Locality remains locality in independent \((U,J,B)\). No locality or
  quasilocality after the physical specialization
  \((U,J)=(U_{k+1}(W),J_{k+1}(W))\) follows.
- The exact leading prefactor lost by the accessible RG-II Eq. (1.32) text
  layers remains visually unaudited. Only Note 0013's source-safe consequence
  is used.
- Note 0021 subsequently proves the exact cutoff-conditioned marked algebra
  on one fixed compatible branch. It does not synchronize the shifted family.
  No convergent marked norm, marginal projection, profile mixing theorem,
  large-field estimate, RG iteration, continuum construction, infrared decay,
  or mass gap follows.

## Falsification checks

- Allow an arbitrary unit shift and verify that it need not preserve
  \(\mathbb T^{(k+1)}\), so the cited RG-I symmetry class and transformation
  discussion do not license its use here.
- Set \(m=1\), hence \(n=1\), and place a plaquette on an allowed wall; the
  positivity conclusion in (4) fails.
- Replace \(n_p\) in (7) by one position-independent count and check that the
  exact average or the covariance normalization can fail.
- Replace the normalized average by an unnormalized sum and recover the
  missing factor \(n_p\) in (10).
- Use a single-shift selection rule and do not call it
  \(\Gamma_{N,L}\)-equivariant unless its behavior under signed permutations
  and reflections is separately proved.
- Use an unsymmetrized generalized-random-walk expansion and expose the gap
  between undecoupled RG-I covariance and termwise weakened covariance.
- Insert all shift labels into one fixed-partition Section-2 cluster sum and
  identify the missing compatibility theorem.
- Substitute the minimizing background and reject any inference of strict
  coarse-field locality.
