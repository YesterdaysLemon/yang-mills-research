# Frozen problem specification

Version: 2026-07-22

Canonical statement: [Jaffe–Witten, *Quantum Yang–Mills Theory*](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf)

## Required theorem

For each compact simple gauge group \(G\), one must construct a nontrivial pure quantum Yang–Mills theory on full four-dimensional Euclidean/Minkowski space, establish axiomatic properties at least as strong as the Wightman/Osterwalder–Schrader frameworks cited in the official statement, match the required short-distance Yang–Mills behavior, and prove that the reconstructed Hamiltonian has a finite positive mass gap.

## Obligation matrix

| Obligation | What must be established | Current state |
|---|---|---|
| Gauge-group scope | Every compact simple \(G\), or a complete proved reduction | Open |
| Continuum existence | A regulator-free QFT on \(\mathbb R^4\) | Open |
| Infinite volume | Control beyond a torus or finite box | Open |
| Nontriviality | Nonzero, non-Gaussian Yang–Mills local content | Open |
| Local observables | Gauge-invariant local fields associated with curvature and covariant derivatives | Open |
| Ultraviolet behavior | Compatibility with asymptotic freedom and perturbative renormalization at short distance | Open |
| Axioms | Wightman- or Osterwalder–Schrader-strength properties and reconstruction | Open |
| Spectral claim | \(\sigma(H)\cap(0,\Delta_G)=\varnothing\) for some finite \(\Delta_G>0\) | Open |

The gap may depend on \(G\). The statement does not demand a single numerical lower bound uniform over all compact simple groups.

## Explicit non-solutions

None of the following, by itself, satisfies the target:

- classical Yang–Mills PDE existence or special classical fields;
- a formal path integral or perturbative renormalizability;
- Monte Carlo evidence or a numerical glueball mass;
- a fixed lattice spacing, finite lattice, or strong-coupling lattice phase;
- a finite-volume gap without uniform control through the limits;
- weak subsequential compactness without identification, axioms, nontriviality, and gap;
- a theory only in two or three dimensions;
- an abelian, supersymmetric, Higgs, or matter-coupled substitute;
- a proof for only one gauge group;
- confinement used as a slogan rather than the requested spectral theorem.

Confinement, chiral symmetry breaking, full QCD, and construction on arbitrary four-manifolds are important neighboring questions but are not extra clauses of the official prize statement.

## Acceptance is separate from correctness

A mathematically complete proof would still not immediately be a Clay-recognized solution. Under the [official rules](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/millennium_prize_rules_0.pdf), the work must appear in a qualifying outlet, at least two years must pass, it must achieve general acceptance after rigorous examination, and Clay must complete its own evaluation. Essential proof steps therefore cannot be hidden in repository-only supplements.
